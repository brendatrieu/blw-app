If you take one nutrition priority away from this whole app, make it this one: **iron is the nutrient to build meals around starting at 6 months.** Everything else — variety, textures, allergen introduction — matters too, but iron has a timing urgency the others don't.

## Why iron specifically, and why starting at 6 months

Babies are typically born with iron stores built up during pregnancy, largely in the last trimester. Those stores are enough to cover their needs for roughly the first 6 months of life, which is part of why breastmilk alone (which is naturally low in iron) is sufficient nutrition up to that point. But by around 6 months, those prenatal stores begin to run low right as rapid brain development and growth are placing heavy demands on iron supply — which is exactly why solid foods are introduced at this age, and why iron-rich foods specifically are prioritized from the very first bites.

Iron deficiency in infancy is linked to effects on cognitive and motor development, some of which research suggests may not be fully reversible even after the deficiency is corrected. That's the reason this app leads with iron-rich foods and iron-focused recipes rather than treating iron as one nutrient among many — the window where it matters most is narrow and early.

## Heme iron vs. non-heme iron

Not all dietary iron behaves the same way in the body, and understanding the difference helps explain some of the recipe pairings you'll see in this app.

**Heme iron** comes from animal sources — beef, chicken (especially dark meat like thigh), salmon, sardines, and other meat and fish. It's absorbed efficiently by the body on its own, generally in the range of 15–35% of what's eaten, without needing anything else to help it along.

**Non-heme iron** comes from plant sources — lentils, chickpeas, black beans, tofu, iron-fortified infant oats, spinach, and quinoa. It's absorbed far less efficiently on its own, often in the single digits percentage-wise, and it's also more easily blocked by certain compounds in the same meal (like the tannins in tea, or excess calcium at the same sitting).

This is where vitamin C comes in.

## The vitamin C pairing trick

**Vitamin C dramatically boosts non-heme iron absorption when eaten in the same meal.** Pairing a plant-based iron source with a vitamin-C-rich food at the same sitting can meaningfully increase how much of that non-heme iron actually gets absorbed, compared to eating the iron source alone.

Good vitamin-C pairing foods include broccoli, bell pepper, strawberry, orange, kiwi, mango, and tomato.

**A few pairing examples:**

- Lentils + bell pepper strips
- Iron-fortified oats + mashed strawberry or kiwi
- Black beans + a small wedge of orange or tomato
- Spinach mixed into a dish alongside broccoli or a vitamin-C side

This is exactly why you'll see iron-rich foods in this app explicitly linked to vitamin-C pairing suggestions — it's not a random flourish, it's a deliberate absorption strategy baked into the recipe and food catalog design. Heme-iron foods (meat, fish) don't need this pairing to absorb well, though there's no harm in serving them together with vitamin-C foods too.

## Building iron into everyday meals

A few practical habits that help keep iron consistently in the rotation:

- **Lead with an iron anchor at most meals** — a small serving of meat, fish, egg, or a legume/iron-fortified grain, rather than treating iron-rich food as an occasional special ingredient
- **Pair plant-based iron sources with a vitamin-C food** in the same sitting whenever you can
- **Don't rely on fruit and vegetables alone to carry a meal's nutrition** — they're valuable for fiber, vitamins, and vitamin C specifically, but they're not meaningful iron sources on their own
- **Iron-fortified infant cereals and oats** are a genuinely useful iron source in early months, especially before your baby is eating large volumes of meat or legumes

## It's a pattern, not a per-meal grade

No single meal needs to be nutritionally perfect, and there's no need to track grams or percentages. What matters is the overall pattern across days and weeks — regularly offering iron-rich foods, regularly pairing the plant-based ones with vitamin C, and not worrying if one particular lunch was mostly about your baby smearing avocado around the high chair tray instead of eating it (a very normal part of the learning process).
